# InstantJPDict 1.0.0-rc5 — tester notes

Thank you for trying this release candidate. It is feature-complete but not
final: expect rough edges, and please say what you find.

## What it is

An offline Japanese dictionary that reads the Japanese already on your screen.
Turn on its accessibility service, trigger a capture, and tap a word to see
readings, meanings, pitch accents and examples. You can also share a screenshot
or photo to it, or point its camera at printed text. Everything runs on the
device: no account, no telemetry.

Captures come from the phone's own surfaces — double-press volume-down, the
accessibility button, or the accessibility shortcut — so nothing of ours floats
over other apps. See *How to trigger a capture* below.

## What you need

- Android 11 or newer, on a 64-bit ARM (arm64-v8a) device. Most phones from the
  last several years qualify.
- The app is about 38 MB; the recommended dictionary is another ~37 MB.
- An internet connection for the one-time dictionary download.

## Setup (two steps)

1. **Install a dictionary first.** Open the app, tap **Dictionary catalog**,
   then **Install** beside **Jitendex** (recommended). **KANJIDIC (English)**
   adds kanji readings and meanings if you want them.
2. **Turn on the overlay.** Tap **Enable accessibility service** and try to
   enable **InstantJPDict** in the list. Try this **first**: on Android 13+,
   **Allow restricted settings** only appears *after* a blocked attempt. If
   you were stopped by an *"App was denied access"* popup, go to system
   settings → Apps → InstantJPDict → three-dot menu → **Allow restricted
   settings**, then come back and enable the service again. The app's
   **"App was denied access"?** link repeats these steps.

Enabling the service from Settings does **not** scan the screen — that is
deliberate, so that turning it on and configuring it never fires a capture you
did not ask for. Once it is on, use one of the triggers below.

## How to trigger a capture

Any of these works while the app's service is enabled:

- **Double-press the volume-down button.** One capture per double-press, and a
  single press still changes the volume as usual. This is the one to reach for.
- **The accessibility button** — the small on-screen shortcut Android offers
  (Settings → Accessibility → InstantJPDict → **Accessibility button**): one
  capture per tap.
- **The accessibility shortcut** (Settings → Accessibility → **Shortcut**; on a
  Pixel: hold both volume keys, or the two-finger swipe up from the bottom
  edge). This one **toggles the service**, so every other press captures: the
  press that switches the service off cannot also scan. The triggers above are
  the ones where every press works.

There is nothing to drag and nothing to hide: the service stays on after a
capture, and stays on until you switch it off yourself.

## Closing the overlay

Tap the **←** in the top-left corner, press Back, tap empty space, or swipe down
from the status strip. The overlay closes one layer at a time — the dictionary
panel first, the overlay second — and the service stays enabled throughout.

## Permissions, and why

- **Accessibility service** — reads what is on screen to find Japanese text. It
  is what the overlay is. Nothing leaves your device.
- **Camera** — camera mode only; nothing is captured until you press the shutter.
- **Internet** — the dictionary catalog's downloads. The dictionary itself works
  offline.

## Changes since 1.0.0-rc4

- **A hardening pass over the text paths.** A security review of the two places
  arbitrary text enters the app — OCR output and the text an app exposes to the
  accessibility service — found and fixed every crash point it could reach,
  including two ways a hostile app could freeze the main thread with a very
  large text tree. An adversarial test corpus now covers that whole surface.
  Nothing you should notice; that is the point.
- **Cursor navigation was rebuilt.** The graph that maps up/down/left/right
  between characters now scales with the page instead of the square of it —
  20,000 characters build in about 0.3 s where the old code ran out of memory
  at 10,000 — and it is genuinely connected on sparse pages, so navigation no
  longer dead-ends where the old graph was broken.
- **Experimental: reading text from the app itself.** Where an app exposes its
  text through Android's accessibility tree, InstantJPDict can use that text
  instead of recognising it from pixels, drawing its overlay over the app's own
  glyphs; OCR fills in the rest. It is **off by default** — **Debug settings**
  has the two tree-sourced modes, both marked experimental. It is the roughest
  part of this candidate, which is why it is opt-in: expect the occasional
  misplaced line while it matures.

## New in 1.0.0-rc4 (unchanged, and still worth knowing)

- **The accessibility button responds immediately.** Every trigger used to wait
  a fixed 300 ms before asking for the screenshot — a wait that only *turning
  the service on* actually needs — and the button was paying it for nothing,
  along with a window-list read that only the Settings check needed. It now
  waits the 50 ms the old floating button did, and that read has left the path
  entirely. If the button felt sluggish next to the old one, that pause is gone.
- **The floating 辞典 button is gone.** Capture comes from the phone's own
  surfaces only; the service stays enabled after a capture.

## Known limitations

- **Vertical text** (manga, tategaki) and unusual layouts are the weak spot:
  expect misreads or missing results there.
- Stylised fonts and overlapping text can split lines or merge words.
- OCR is fast but not perfect; a single misread character can change the result.
- **Ruby (furigana)** can come back as its own result: the filter that drops it
  is off by default in this build. **Debug settings** has per-mode switches
  (screenshots / camera) if you want it on.
- The **accessibility-shortcut** trigger alternates on and off — that is the
  platform's toggle, not a bug; use the volume double-press or the accessibility
  button when you want every press to scan.
- The **experimental tree-sourced modes** can misdraw a line an app renders at
  several font sizes in one row; that is the known rough edge while the feature
  matures.

## How to report

The quickest path is the **Have Feedback?** button at the bottom of the app's
home screen. It opens your mail app with
**holographicpengin+InstantJPDict@gmail.com** already in the To field, and the
app version and device pre-filled.

Or write to that address yourself, with:

- phone model and Android version,
- what you were looking at (a screenshot helps),
- what you expected, and what happened instead.

Crashes, wrong lookups, and "the overlay didn't appear" are all useful. If the
app crashes, it offers to email the same address with the crash log attached.
